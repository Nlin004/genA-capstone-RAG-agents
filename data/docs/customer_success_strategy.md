# Customer Success Strategy

## Purpose

This document outlines how the Customer Success (CS) team helps customers
get value from the product, renew, and expand their usage over time.

## Segmentation

Customers are segmented into three tiers by annual contract value: Growth
accounts (self-serve, low-touch, driven by in-app guidance and email
nurture), Mid-Market accounts (a shared CS manager for a book of ~40
accounts, quarterly check-ins), and Enterprise accounts (a dedicated CS
manager, a named executive sponsor, and a formal quarterly business
review).

## Onboarding

Every new customer is assigned an onboarding plan with a target
time-to-first-value of 14 days. Enterprise and Mid-Market accounts get a
kickoff call within the first week; Growth accounts are guided through an
in-app checklist. Onboarding completion and time-to-first-value are
tracked as core CS metrics.

## Health Scoring and Churn Prevention

Each account has a health score derived from product usage frequency,
feature adoption breadth, support ticket volume and sentiment, and NPS
responses. Accounts that drop into the "at risk" band trigger an
automatic playbook: a check-in call within five business days and a
tailored re-engagement plan. Sales is looped in only if a renewal
conversation is imminent.

## Expansion

CS managers identify expansion opportunities (seat growth, upsell to a
higher plan, add-on modules) during quarterly business reviews and hand
qualified opportunities to the sales team rather than closing them
directly, keeping a clean separation between relationship management and
commercial negotiation.

## Alignment With Product and Engineering

CS aggregates recurring customer feedback into a quarterly themes report
shared with Product, and flags any feedback related to policy, security,
or process (for example, requests tied to our security or complaint
handling policies) directly to the relevant policy owner so strategy and
policy stay consistent with what customers are actually experiencing.
